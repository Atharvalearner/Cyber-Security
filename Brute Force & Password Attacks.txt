Brute Force & Password Attacks : 
- Tools : John the Ripper, Hydra, Medusa, Hashcat
- Types 1. Brute Force Attack:
		Attacker tries all possible password combinations until correct.
		Its effectiveness depends on password length, complexity, rate limits, the hashing algorithm, and available computing resources.
		Long passwords, MFA, rate limiting, and strong password hashing help reduce the risk.
		Example Guessing ATM PIN by trying all 0000–9999 combinations.

	2. Dictionary Attack :
		Tries common wordspasswords from a list.
		Example Trying “password123” or “qwerty” before random guesses.

	3. Credential Stuffing :
		Uses previously stolen username and password combinations from one breach to attempt access to other services
		Example If your Netflix password leaks, hacker tries same password on Gmail.

	4. Password Spraying :
		Tries common passwords across many accounts (instead of one account).
		Example Testing “Welcome@123” on 1000 employee accounts.

	5. Keylogging :
		Malware records everything you type.
		Example Keylogger captures your Facebook password as you type.

	6. A rainbow table:
		a precomputed data structure used to speed up the recovery of passwords from certain unsalted password hashes.
		Instead of calculating everything from the beginning each time, precomputed information is used.
		

# Why is Salt Important?
Suppose two users choose: Password123
Without proper salting, they may produce identical password hashes under the same scheme.
